Overview

This class aims to introduce the main theoretical foundations of asymptotic statistics.

Room: 1TP1 BMIG.

Evaluation

For all students, the final grade is entirely based on the exam.

Exam syllabus Since the first blockade on October 12, every session has been disrupted. You are therefore expected to know the class only up to that point, that is Lectures 1 to 3. For the rest, the slides are online: you are expected to read and understand them, but not to know their content perfectly. If the exam has questions on these parts, they will come as a guided problem with intermediate questions, and you will never be asked to regurgitate a result from the class.
To practise, you can use the self-assessment and the exams of previous years: 2018 and 2019.

References and External Resources

Lecture notes from previous editions of the class by François Bachoc.

Statistics

  • Asymptotic Statistics by A. W. van der Vaart, 2000.

Measure and Probability Theory

  • Intégration, Probabilités et Processus Aléatoires by Jean-François Le Gall, 2006: An excellent introduction to measure theory with elements of stochastic processes and conditional expectation.
  • Probabilités 2 by Jean-Yves Ouvrard, 2009: A detailed treatment of advanced topics in probability theory.
  • Exercices de probabilités by M. Cottrell, C. Duhamel, V. Genon-Catalot, T. Meyre, 2016.
  • Concentration Inequalities: A Nonasymptotic Theory of Independence by Stéphane Boucheron, Gábor Lugosi, and Pascal Massart, 2013.

Machine Learning and Learning Theory

  • Learning Theory from First Principles by Francis Bach, 2024.

Analysis

  • Cours d'analyse by Jean-Michel Bony, 2001.
