  • June 2026 Our article Learning with Differentially Private Sliced Wasserstein Gradients just got accepted for publication in the journal Transactions on Machine Learning Research. Huge thanks to my coauthors David Rodríguez-Vítores and Jean-Michel Loubes, and to the reviewers for their suggestions.
    Try it: training an encoder on private data with differentially private sliced Wasserstein gradients.
    Along the dashed direction: encoded data (top) matched in sorted order with the prior (bottom)
    private trainingsame run without privacy
    Dark dots: the private data after encoding; orange dots: the public prior N(0, I). At each step, the gradient of the sliced Wasserstein distance is computed along a few random directions, where optimal transport simply matches sorted values. The data and the encoded points are clipped, and Gaussian noise calibrated to the gradient's sensitivity is added before each Adam update, so that the whole training is (ε, 10−5)-differentially private. Our main result bounds this sensitivity by O(1/n): with more data, privacy costs less noise, and the private encoder gets close to the non-private one.
